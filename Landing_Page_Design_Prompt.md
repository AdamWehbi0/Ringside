Design a single-page marketing landing page for a SaaS product called Lineup (placeholder name — swap in the real brand name before using this anywhere final). It sells an AI phone-and-text ordering system to independent restaurants and small multi-location restaurant groups.

## Audience and tone

The buyer is a restaurant owner-operator, not a technical buyer — busy, skeptical of tech sales pitches, cares about lost revenue from missed calls more than feature lists. Tone should be warm, direct, and credible: modern food-tech aesthetic, not corporate SaaS, not gimmicky or overly playful. Confident and plainspoken over clever. Avoid stock-photo-of-people-in-suits energy — this should feel like it was made by people who've actually worked a restaurant counter.

## Visual direction

Warm, appetizing palette — think cream/parchment background, deep charcoal or near-black text, one confident warm accent color (terracotta, burnt orange, or a deep red) for CTAs and highlights, and an optional deep forest green or teal as a secondary anchor color. Editorial serif for headlines paired with a clean modern sans-serif for body text works well for this space — food-forward but still legible as software. Generous whitespace, confident typography scale, no clutter. If icons are used, simple line-icon style, not cartoonish. A phone/text conversation visual (a stylized message bubble mockup showing a short sample order exchange) would help ground the abstract pitch in something concrete.

## Page structure and exact content

**1. Hero section**
Headline: "Every call answered. Every order paid before it hits the kitchen."
Subhead: "An AI phone and text line built for restaurants — takes orders, answers questions, or gets someone on the line, 24 hours a day. No missed calls, no walk-away pickups."
Three small stat callouts: "24/7 coverage, including the rush you can't staff for" / "$0 setup fee, month-to-month, no contract" / "Paid before every order reaches your kitchen"

**2. Problem section**
Header: "The phone is still where restaurants lose money"
Subhead: "Every unanswered call during a dinner rush is an order that just went to the place down the street instead."
Three pain points:
- Missed calls, missed orders — "Your staff is running food, not answering a phone that rings during your busiest 20 minutes."
- Walk-away pickups — "Phone orders paid at pickup mean food gets made and never claimed. That's real, avoidable food cost."
- No idea what you're actually losing — "Without call logs, a missed-call problem is invisible until you go looking for it."

**3. Pricing section** (three plans, side by side, middle one visually highlighted as "Most Popular")

*Order Line* — "Bare-bones ordering. Nothing else." — $1.50 per order, $49/month minimum. Includes: voice + SMS order-taking, payment collected before POS, direct POS integration, order-confirmation text included, non-order calls forwarded to the restaurant's existing line.

*Full Line* (Most Popular badge) — "Run the phone like a real channel." — $1.50 per order, $150/month minimum. Includes everything in Order Line, plus: answers menu/hours/allergen questions, transfers to front desk on request, 350 question/transfer calls/month included, full analytics dashboard (containment rate, average order value, busiest hours), self-serve menu and hours editing, multiple staff logins, optional SMS win-back campaign add-on.

*Multi-Location* — "3+ locations, one account." — $1.25 per order per location, $139/month minimum per location, 3-location minimum commitment. Includes everything in Full Line, plus: one roll-up dashboard across all locations, pooled question/transfer call allowance, dedicated account support, priority build-out for a new POS system.

**4. Trust section**
A single honest line, not a fabricated testimonial: "Built by someone who actually runs the counter — not a software company that's never worked a dinner rush. We operate our own restaurant locations and built this to solve our own missed-call problem first." Do not invent customer testimonials, logos, or "trusted by X restaurants" claims — there are no paying customers yet. Leave a placeholder note that real performance numbers go here once the pilot is complete.

**5. FAQ section** (five items)
- "What if I have a slow month?" — "The monthly minimum is sized to cover our actual cost of keeping your line running, nothing more. You're never charged extra just for being quiet that month — and once you're above that floor, you're simply billed per order."
- "Do I have to sign a contract?" — "No. Month-to-month on every plan, no setup fee for early customers, cancel anytime."
- "Why does payment happen before the order reaches my kitchen?" — "So you never make food for someone who doesn't show up. It removes walk-away pickups entirely."
- "What if my POS isn't supported yet?" — "We integrate directly with Toast, Clover, and Square today. If you're on something else, tell us — Multi-Location customers get priority build-out, and we're expanding coverage based on real demand."
- "Is this just a chatbot?" — "No — it's a phone and text line that actually talks with your customers, takes real orders with modifiers and substitutions, and knows your specific menu, hours, and policies."

**6. Final CTA section**
Header: "See it running on your own menu"
Subhead: "No commitment to look at it. We'll set it up against your actual menu so you can hear it take a real order before you decide anything."
Button: contact placeholder (email or phone, to be filled in)

## Technical requirements

Single responsive page, mobile-friendly, works as a static HTML/CSS page (or whatever native output format the tool produces). Clear, high-contrast CTA buttons. The "Most Popular" plan should be visually distinct (border, elevation, or badge) without breaking the three-column layout on desktop. Keep all pricing and FAQ text fully readable — this needs to function as a real page people make purchasing decisions from, not a purely decorative visual.
